# Problem 2 – Winning the voluntary switch to SF6-free switchgear

## The problem

In the EU, regulation is phasing SF6 out of new medium-voltage switchgear, so customers there must switch. In markets with no SF6 rule (most of APAC, the Middle East and the Americas), nothing forces them to. There, RM AirSeT (SF6-free) costs more than RM6 (SF6) and gives Schneider a thinner margin, so it has to win on its own merits.

The client asks: **which markets, value proposition, pricing and adoption levers accelerate the switch profitably**, and how the raw-material forecast of Problem 1 supports the argument.

## Our approach

We state six hypotheses about why customers would switch (global ESG standards, coming regulation, lifetime cost, raw-material convergence, pricing room, repeating the EU price convergence) and test each one with sourced evidence and a customer-side cost model. The pricing and the roadmap are then built per segment, and checked against Schneider's margin using Problem 1's raw-material risk.

| **5. Raw-material outlook** | Uses Problem 1's forecast to test whether the RM6 − AirSeT raw-material cost gap narrows by mid-2027. | [02](02_cost_and_tco.md) |
| **6. Value proposition** | Writes one message per target segment, and lists what we deliberately don't claim. | [03](03_value_and_pricing.md) |
| **7. Pricing** | Finds each segment's price window: the most the customer should accept, and the least that keeps Schneider's margin after Problem 1's raw-material risk. | [03](03_value_and_pricing.md) |
| **8. Adoption levers** | Maps who must be convinced, the levers that convince them, and the barriers. | [03](03_value_and_pricing.md) |

**Where Problem 1 comes in:** the raw-material cost per unit (Problem 1 step 2) in the cost gap, the cost outlook (step 8) in the convergence test, and the quote rule with its indexation clause (step 9) in the pricing and roadmap.
